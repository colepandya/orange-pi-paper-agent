# Build 56: a new position gets a grace period before an evidence exit applies
Reported: GOOGL (-$20), MSFT (-$42) and AMZN (-$228), all opened at 04:00 and all closed at 04:38 for "RISK_ESCALATION", "such low loss and losing out on potential".
RISK_ESCALATION is not a price stop. It is the position monitor's judgement about the EVIDENCE, and it never looked at the P&L. It is raised by: (1) adverse news since the plan was made (direction <= -0.55 over evidence mass >= 0.15); (2) a "Strong" thesis (probability >= 80%) that works against the position; (3) the largest contributor to a worst-case stress loss above 1.25x the limit. A position at -0.26% and one at -4% were treated identically, and a story or a thesis that touches several holdings closes them all in the same minute. Each such exit also pays the ~0.3% round trip for nothing whenever the signal was a flicker.
* `executor.escalation_waits` / `choose_trigger`: a RISK_ESCALATION exit now waits while the position is younger than `strategy.escalation_grace_minutes` (60) AND its loss is smaller than `strategy.escalation_grace_loss_pct` (1%), judged from the position's own side (a short loses when the price rises). The monitor already clears a signal that is not re-raised within about 12 minutes, so a flicker disappears by itself and only a persistent signal survives the wait; at 60 minutes, or at a 1% loss, it applies as before. The next-highest signal is considered while a higher one waits.
* NOT delayed: stops, targets, time and end-of-day exits, thesis-broken exits, rotation (superior opportunity). With no configuration, or 0 minutes, the old behaviour applies.
* Evidence for the choice is thin and honest: the 6 Nov export holds only 3 RISK_ESCALATION exits (after two the price kept falling, so exiting was right; after one it rose 4%), so this is a design judgement about flickers, not a proven gain. The stop (3%) still bounds the loss during the wait. Strategy version S15.
* `exit_reasons.py` prints the stated reason behind every recent exit.

# Build 55: the risk engine stops rejecting what it should queue, and the EV model says what the OTHER side is worth
Requested: "most of it is Risk Rejected" and "the EV model is rejecting all ideas we should be utilising: use short or put options for these".
**Throughput (requested: "at least 30 trades a day").** What 30 a day means on $1M with 3-day holds: about 90 positions open at once, friction about 0.3% of every dollar traded ($450 a day at 0.5% size, $900 at 1%, $1,800 at 2%) if the signal has NO edge, and about 0.33% gross edge per trade just to net +$50 a day (which is only 0.005% a day, about 1.3% a year). A signal-free trade earned -0.15% to -0.48% after costs in the lab, and the engine supplies about 67 distinct stock ideas a day (about 11 pass the relaxed gates before safety checks). So 30 is a TARGET, never a quota: no trade is forced, and a high-frequency book is only safe with guardrails. This build raises the headroom and adds the guardrails:
* `portfolio.daily_trade_target` default 10 -> 30 (shown on the Overview; up to 200); `risk.position_slots` default 20 -> 40 (no new position takes more than the invested limit / 40, about 2.4%, before the multipliers that only reduce it).
* **The small-trade tier pauses itself** (`risk.low_edge_breaker_trades` 30, `risk.low_edge_breaker_pct` 0.5%): when the last 30 closed small trades have lost more than 0.5% of equity, no new small trade opens until the record recovers (RISK_REJECTED "the small-trade tier is paused ..."). The normal tier is untouched. 0 or a negative number switches it off.
* **A "What happened to the ideas (last 24 hours)" section on the Overview:** every outcome with its count and share, and for RISK_REJECTED, AWAITING_CAPACITY, REJECTED, VETOED and DUPLICATE the most common reasons with counts and shares of that outcome (the same numbers as `breakdown.py`).
* **Audit trail:** `positions.shares` is the REMAINING quantity (a target halves it, a close sets it to 0 and cost to 0), so a closed position could not say how big it had been. New columns `entry_shares` and `entry_cost` (schema 11) record the original size when a position opens; older rows are filled in from their decision on first open. No trade ever executed with zero shares: entry computes `floor(amount / fill)` and defers the order if that is under 1.
* Two bugs the tests caught while building this: a variable named `paused` shadowed the module's `paused()` function and made EVERY idea error; and `table()` already escapes cell text, so escaping first showed `&lt;=` on the page (the Build 54 research tables had the same latent pattern, now fixed).
**Risk engine.** In a simulated market under the shipped defaults 17% of decisions were RISK_REJECTED, almost all for two reasons: "capacity full and every holding is too new to rotate out" and the correlation-twin block. The first is not a risk limit failing; it is a temporary capacity condition that was handled as a terminal rejection.
* Capacity is now a WAIT. `opportunity.evaluate` labels each outcome `kind`: "capacity" (no room, every holding too new, candidate not superior) is queued as AWAITING_CAPACITY and retried every cycle while the idea is fresh (and rotates in when something frees up); "edge" (the cash-hurdle test: the idea does not beat cash) is now a plain REJECTED (an EV verdict, not a portfolio risk limit). RISK_REJECTED remains for the real limits: a broken thesis, the drawdown and daily-loss breakers, the correlation twin, the position monitor, the red team.
* A holding whose remaining edge is gone (remaining EV <= 0) can be rotated out at any age; the 6-hour protection now covers only healthy new positions.
* **Slot budget** (`risk.position_slots`, default 20): no new position takes more than the invested limit divided by this (95% / 20 = 4.75%, before the multipliers that only reduce it). A book of a few 7-8% positions had no room for the next idea; with 10 trades a day and 3-day holds it fills in a day.
* The smallest position worth opening is now 0.5% (was 1%).
**EV model.** A review of the 385 resolved forecasts found: the model's predicted move DOES carry information (correlation 0.38; realised about 63% of the predicted size), and, split by cause, forecasts of a fall after BULLISH news on a stock that had run up ("sell the news", the model's priced-in term) came true at about 60% of the predicted size (about 95% when the run-up was 8%+: predicted -14.3%, realised -13.5%, 16 events), while a bearish headline flipped to a predicted BOUNCE did not (predicted +8.5%, realised -2.1%). **This corrects an earlier note in this project that the priced-in term was "about ten times too large": that compared a news-conditional forecast with the unconditional drift after a rise.** Caveats: one week (28 Oct - 2 Nov), small samples, magnitudes vary by event type (bankruptcy and M&A falls came true; regulatory-action, guidance-cut and cyber-incident falls realised 0-20% of the prediction).
* Good news that the model expects to FALL is no longer just "EV negative, buying loses money". The rejection now says what the other side is worth: "the model expects a FALL: a short or put would earn about +x% after costs and a 60% haircut", or "the opposite side does not pay either". Only this direction is shown (a bearish headline never gets a bounce side).
* Sell-the-news puts ship ON with evidence-based calibration: threshold 3% (was 8%), haircut 0.6 (was 0.5; realised/predicted was 0.63), contract-level EV test, half size, one per stock, self-pausing breaker unchanged. Puts, not stock shorts, because the squeeze rule (never short a stock up 20% in 5 days) blocks exactly the strongest cases and a put's loss is capped.
* Strategy version S14. NOT changed: the priced-in term itself, the correlation twin block, drawdown and daily-loss breakers, sizing for ordinary ideas apart from the slot budget.
* A setting you saved is never overwritten by a release: if you already set `instruments.options_reversal_min_move` (e.g. to 10%) the new 3% default will not apply until you reset it on the Settings page.

# Build 54: a research lab and long price history (observation only)
Why: the bot can judge its own assumptions only on the prices it holds (about a year, one market regime) and on a few dozen trades. A review of the engine against that year found its probabilities are not calibrated (forecasts above 70% came true about 45% of the time; Brier 0.301 against 0.250 for always saying 50%), its "priced in" term is roughly ten times too large (it assumes 80% of a 5-day move reverses; stocks that rose 8-15% gave back 0.55% over 3 sessions and 0.84% over 5), and a stop-and-target plan with no signal loses about 0.4% per trade after costs, so a signal has to add more than that just to break even. None of that can be fixed responsibly without testing on more than one regime. This build adds the machinery; it changes no trade.
* **Long price history** (`history.py`): a bounded per-cycle backfill of `history.years` (5) of daily bars for the stocks the bot tracks (the benchmark first, then the stocks it decides on, then the rest), `history.symbols_per_cycle` (3) at a time, with a time budget, an immediate stop on a rate-limit error, and per-symbol backoff (6, 12, 24, 48 hours, then a week). Idempotent (an upsert). Closes are split-adjusted, not dividend-adjusted.
* **The research lab** (`lab.py`): five studies as tested library code: what stocks do after a 5-day move (against what the forecast assumes), after an earnings reaction, after a big move on heavy volume with no earnings; whether the forecast probabilities come true (Wilson intervals, Brier score, by event type); and what an exit plan earns with no signal. Every return is against SPY; the t-statistic is taken across DAYS; the final 365 days are held out and the split is shown only when both halves have 30+ days; every table counts its comparisons. One study per quiet cycle while the market is closed, weekly, with a time limit; a study too slow for the machine is retried and then left to `research run`.
* **Surfaces:** a read-only "Research lab" section on the Intelligence tab, the `research` command (`report` | `status` | `run [--study NAME]` | `history [--limit N]`), seven Settings (Research lab), schema version 10 (`history_status`, `research_runs`), two cycle stages.
* Observation only: the new settings are excluded from the strategy fingerprint; a test runs a full simulated cycle with the stages on and off and compares every decision.
* On the 6 Nov export the five studies run in about 1 second and 38 MB. Everything printed there is a LEAD, not a result: one regime, small buckets, many comparisons. A finding is a reason to test an idea going forward in the shadow book, never a reason to trade it.
* NOT changed: any trading rule, size, stop or setting.

# Build 53: more trades a day, with the ones that have the least edge kept small
Request: "a minimum of 10 trades a day". On the 6 Nov export the bot made about 1.5 distinct trades a day (the 13 and 9 executions on busy days were inflated by the stacked buys fixed in Build 47). Four gates stood between ideas and trades: the EV minimum (0.6%), the swing reward:risk minimum (1.0), the cash hurdle (cash rate over the horizon plus a hard-coded 0.2% margin, about 0.25-0.33%) and the eligibility score (65). Counting distinct stock-day ideas with their own horizons, the supply BEFORE the safety checks is: 1.5 a day as configured; 5.2 with reward:risk at 0.4; 9.2 with the EV minimum at 0; 10.7 with the cash-hurdle margin at 0.1%. The eligibility score contributes under 1 a day (it was the wrong lever).
* **New defaults:** EV minimum 0.6% -> 0 (negative-EV ideas are still rejected); reward:risk minimum 1.0 -> 0.4 (the blocked ideas sat at 0.5-1.0 with a median EV of 1.0%); cash-hurdle margin 0.2% -> 0.1% (now a Setting, `risk.cash_hurdle_margin`).
* **Low-edge ideas are traded SMALL.** An idea with an expected value under 0.6% (`risk.full_size_min_ev`) or a target under 1.0x its stop (`risk.full_size_min_rr`) is capped at 0.5% of equity (`risk.low_edge_max_pct`), because each round trip costs about 0.3% of what is traded and these ideas have the least edge to pay for it. Ten a day at the old 4% size would be about $1,200 a day of friction; at 0.5% it is about $150. A low-edge position may be smaller than the usual 1% minimum (floor 0.25%); the capacity test accepts that (`opportunity.evaluate(min_pct=...)`: without this the cap would have been silently defeated by the "no capacity" branch). Raising the cap never makes a position harder to open.
* **Visible:** a "Trades today: n / 10" tile on the Overview (`portfolio.daily_trade_target`; a TARGET, not a quota: no trade is forced, so a quiet news day can fall short), and a "By size tier" table on Performance so the low-edge trades are judged on their own record. Every low-edge decision records why (`low_edge`).
* Strategy version S13. NOT changed: the eligibility score (65), the other quality bars, sizing for ordinary ideas, stops. NOT shown: that the extra trades make money. The shadow book found the EV estimate does not rank ideas, so the extra trades are expected to roughly break even before costs; the size cap is what bounds the cost of finding out.

# Build 52: fix a latent "database is locked" race (reported from the Pi)
The Pi reported `gaps: OperationalError: database is locked [gaps.py:13 _task <- gaps.py:34 scan]`. Cause, reproduced deterministically: the research-gap scan WROTE to the database inside a loop that was still READING from it (`for e in conn.execute("SELECT ...")`). In WAL mode that fails INSTANTLY, with no waiting (the 60-second busy timeout does not apply), if another process commits a write between the read and the write; the guard, the executor and the dashboard all write. It is old code, not a Build 49-51 change. Harmless to trading (a research-task scanner that runs again next cycle) but a real bug.
* The four loops that did it (three in `gaps.py`, one in `flows.record_event_flows`) now read their rows fully (`.fetchall()`) before writing.
* A static guard test fails the build if ANY module reintroduces a loop that writes while reading from an open cursor, and race tests commit from a second connection mid-loop. A scan of every module found no other instance, including loops that hand the connection to helpers (all read-only).
* No trading, sizing, matching or schema change.

# Build 51: reversal puts (the model's own forecast of a fall) and a test of whether that forecast is any good
Found from a screenshot of the Decisions tab: NVDA rejected over and over with "EV net -8.21%: buying would lose money". Those are BULLISH headlines on a stock that has already risen. The forecast subtracts what is "priced in" (`priced_in = 0.8 * the stock's 5-day move in the news direction`, capped at 16%) and when that exceeds the news's expected size the residual flips sign: the model predicts the stock FALLS (here about 8% in 3 sessions). The stock rules then stopped at "buying loses money" and never acted on that forecast, because a short was only ever considered when the headline itself was bearish.
* **Reversal puts (OFF by default).** A bullish idea whose forecast is a fall of at least 8% net of costs (`options_reversal_min_move`) becomes a PUT, not a stock short (no squeeze or borrow risk). Brakes: the predicted move is cut by half (`options_reversal_haircut`; the overshoot model is unvalidated); the actual contract must still be expected to return at least 10% on its premium after decay and the exit spread; half size (0.6% of equity); one option per stock and a 24-hour cooldown after a reversal put closes; it shares the self-pausing breaker with the trial and rescue tiers; every other safety check still applies. The position monitor's "fighting a strong thesis" test is not applied, because the monitor does not manage options and the conflict is the point. The forecast row keeps its original verdict. Five Settings. **It ships OFF**, because the evidence is not there: see below.
* **The shadow book now scores the model's OWN forecast direction** (a new table on the Intelligence tab and in `shadow`): when the model predicted a big move, did the stock go that way, market-adjusted and after cost, one observation per stock and calendar week (consecutive days overlap). First readout on the 6 Nov export (ONE week, a falling AI-stock market): forecasts of a FALL of 8%+ were followed by +4.4% (n=29, 95% interval -0.4% to +12.3%: includes zero; the model said 13%); a fall of 4-8%: +0.5%, nothing; forecasts of a RISE of 8%+ were followed by -5.7% (the stock kept FALLING; 95% -13.5% to -0.2%). Both big forecasts being followed by falls is what a selloff looks like, not model skill, and six groups were compared at once. An early draft of this note quoted +7.2% with an interval excluding zero: that double-counted overlapping days.
* Strategy version S12 (the reversal path is off by default, so decisions are unchanged until it is switched on). NOT changed: the "priced in" model itself, sizing, stops.

# Build 43  (strategy S8): bug fixes, a more accurate reasoning engine, a better SEC facts reader, a gentler red team
* **RELEASE-SCRIPT BUG FOUND BY THE ROLLBACK REHEARSAL (affects every earlier v5 build): rollback could not restore the program.** The installer saved the current code only if the OLD v4 folder `/opt/paper-agent/app` existed, so since v5 began no backup contained the program; `rollback.sh` then deleted `paper_agent` and restored only the database, settings and units, leaving a machine with no program. Fixed: the installer now saves the current program (and stops the install if it cannot), and `rollback.sh` REFUSES, before changing anything, a backup that has no program (every backup made before build 43 is of that kind). Regression tests run the real rollback script in a sandbox. NOTE: a rollback to build 42 is therefore possible only from the backup that the build-43 install itself makes; keep the build-42 ZIP as the fallback.
* **Denial is scoped (analyst and red team).** A denial negates ONE proposition about SPECIFIC companies at a SPECIFIC time. It applies only when it denies the story's own event (not a detail such as the price, not a different proposition such as "wrongdoing", not a past denial or one by a judge), only to the companies it is about (a claimant is not denied by the other side's denial), and not when it was followed by a confirmation (the later statement wins, in either order). The story's state and certainty are read accordingly; the red team reads the analyst's logged scope.
* **SEC twelve-month figures are built only when a reliable construction exists, otherwise the metric is UNAVAILABLE with the reason.** Annual + year-to-date - prior year-to-date (the periods must line up, to within a 52/53-week year, in one unit, with no conflicting duplicates), or four contiguous quarters, or a recent annual alone; never an old annual in place of a recent period. Handled and tested: non-calendar fiscal years, 52/53-week years, a missing prior year-to-date, amendments, duplicate contexts, a change of reporting currency or fiscal year-end, discontinued operations, recast comparatives.
* **Every figure carries its own freshness** (period covered, filing date, retrieval time, age); the valuation also carries the market-value timestamp and refuses a stale one; warnings carry the period and filing of the numbers behind them; `ask why` shows all of it and lists what is unavailable and why.
* **Fundamentals objections must be relevant** to the catalyst and holding period, are capped (at most the three worst, and together at most 15% smaller), are size-only in every mode, with ONE narrow direct-causal exception (a financing or solvency catalyst on severe numbers: a veto in active mode only, never in guard).
* **Red-team refinements measured, not tuned:** the same-announcement window (2 hours) changes only SIZE, never blocking: blocks are 2 and active-would-block 2 for every window from 30 minutes to 8 hours, and 2 and 4 hours give identical results. Several stories saying the same opposite thing now shrink a trade once, not once each.
* **Entity matcher regression found by the old-vs-new comparison and fixed before release:** excluding nationality words as brands also removed the two-word alias that matched "British American Tobacco". The nationality list now stops only the single first-word brand. Across all 9,389 real news items the matcher differs on 61 stories (63 removals, 0 additions): every one a nationality or region word (British, European, Dutch, Norwegian, Mexican, German) matched as a company.
* In OFF mode build 43 is identical to build 42 on all six real-data scenarios.
* **BUG: "dashboard error: attempt to write a readonly database" on `why NVDA` (the Ask box).** The question ran the full red-team review, which saves it to the audit log, on the dashboard's read-only connection. Reviews now take a `persist` flag; a question never writes. A permanent test runs every question type and every tab on a genuinely read-only connection.
* **Bug hunt.** Thousands of hostile and malformed inputs were thrown at every new function. It found and fixed: SEC data with a bad date or null value crashed the facts summary; a single daily bar with a missing high or low aborted the outcome tracker for every prediction; a red-team review of a candidate with a missing or non-numeric field crashed; `graph.derive` crashed on a malformed candidate. All now tolerated, all with permanent tests.
* **Reasoning accuracy, measured.** Two labelled sets (a development set of 76 cases, 8 of them taken from the owner's real data, and a held-out set of 49 written afterwards) now run as tests with accuracy floors. The held-out accuracy rose from 84% to 98%, the development set from 79% to 100%. What changed: a second reader of the wording that understands NEGATION ("did not cut", "avoids bankruptcy"), HEDGING ("might", "if") and HISTORY ("a year ago") clause by clause, with a rule for which reader to trust when they disagree (a downgrade was being read as good news); a neutral band (a faint reading is no direction); expectations are not results; "wins a case against X" makes X the loser; a possible bid still has an acquirer and a target; the story's state comes from the headline.
* **Grounding.** The analyst no longer trusts the entity matcher: a company is the subject only as firmly as its name actually appears in the story (headline 1.0, body 0.7, nowhere 0.35; a platform named in "joins the Microsoft program" counts for little; "(NASDAQ: X)" does not make Nasdaq the company). Question headlines, market-research pages, stock-pick lists, recaps, roundups and statements of optimism are not events.
* **Entity matcher**: nationality and region adjectives ("British", "Chinese"...) are no longer company brands ("Toblerone owner sues British vegan brand" matched British American Tobacco).
* **SEC facts reader v2.** Trailing twelve months the way analysts compute it (annual + year-to-date - prior year-to-date; the fourth quarter derived), growth on a like-for-like footing, the tag a company actually still uses rather than an abandoned one, and more signals: liquidity, interest cover, negative equity, margin squeeze, profit without cash, leverage, a coarse valuation. Stale data is never used; a 403/429 from SEC stops the stage and backs off for an hour; the placeholder contact email is called out.
* **Red team refinements.** A story within two hours of the one an idea came from is the same announcement, not newer news; one company among several named is made smaller (weak subject) instead of vetoed unless another company is the clear subject; at most the three worst fundamentals objections; size multipliers softened until the scorecard has evidence. On the owner's 84 real ideas with the full chain: 2% blocked (both on news over 70 hours old), 71% at full size, average size x0.93; active mode would also block only 2%.
* A denial or rejection mentioned only in the BODY no longer marks a story as denied or flips its direction.
